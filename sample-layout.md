This page is a sample. It shows how an article looks on Chokepoint, and how to add a new one. Delete it once your first real piece is up.

## Adding an article takes two steps

**First,** write your article as a plain text file in the `articles` folder, ending in `.md`. Give it a short name with dashes instead of spaces, such as `why-hormuz-matters-to-india.md`. Write normally; a few simple marks add formatting:

- Two hash signs and a space at the start of a line make a subheading, like the one above.
- Two asterisks on each side make **bold** text; one on each side makes *italics*.
- A line starting with `>` becomes a pull quote.
- `[words](https://example.com)` turns words into a link — use this for your sources.

**Second,** open `articles/articles.json` and add an entry with the same short name as the `slug`, plus the title, a one-line summary (`dek`), the date, a topic `tag` such as *Markets* or *Politics*, and, if the piece first ran in a school publication, its name in `publication`.

> The first paragraph gets a large initial letter automatically, the way a magazine opens a feature.

## Images

Put the picture in the `articles` folder too, then write `![What the picture shows](articles/picture-name.jpg)`. The words in the square brackets are read aloud to people using screen readers, so describe the picture.

## A note on balance

The rest of Chokepoint sticks to sourced facts and treats every side evenly. Your articles can argue a view — that's what an opinion piece is for — and the byline makes clear they're yours. Link your evidence, and readers will trust your argument more.
